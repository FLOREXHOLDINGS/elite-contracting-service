The look

What the inspection or estimate reads.

  • Where the animal is — wall, attic, crawlspace, under a porch, in the yard
  • How it's reached, and what has to be opened to reach it
  • The size, which sets the price tier
  • Contamination left behind, and whether disinfection is part of the job

Where the animal died decides the work. An animal in an open attic is a straightforward removal; an animal in a wall is a locate-and-open job, and the form asks where it is and how it's reached so the trip is planned right the first time.

After the removal, the area can be disinfected as part of the correction — ask when you book, and it goes in the same number.

Straight answers

Asked, and answered plainly.

How do I pick the size?

Small is rodents and the like, medium is skunks and raccoons, large is deer. If you're between sizes, say so — the worse-than-described rule protects you either way.

What does worse than described mean?

If the job is materially worse than what was booked, you pick: a full refund and we don't take the job, or an added charge set on site and agreed before any work starts.

Is the smell gone when the animal is?

The source goes with the animal. Disinfection of the area is available as part of the job — ask when you book.
