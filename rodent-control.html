Mice and rats — out, and the way they got in, closed. No poison. Ever.

The look

What the inspection or estimate reads.

  • Entry points — chewed lattice, foundation gaps, threshold and pipe openings
  • Runs and rub marks along joists, pipes, and wall bases
  • Seed caches and droppings in attic and basement insulation
  • Nesting in wall bays and insulation

The method is traps and exclusion, in that order of proof: the traps are photographed with their catches, and the entry points are photographed before they're closed. You don't take anyone's word that the work happened — you see it.

Poison is refused for a reason you can see in these photos: a poisoned mouse dies where it hides, in a wall or a joist bay, and becomes a dead-animal job and a smell that lasts for weeks. Traps end the problem where it can be reached, and closing the entry ends it for good.

Straight answers

Asked, and answered plainly.

Do you ever use poison or bait?

No. No poison, ever — traps and exclusion only.

What does the rodent inspection cost?

For a homeowner, nothing — the inspection is free. College housing and rental properties are charged the standard $189 inspection fee; commercial properties are charged $245.70.

Why can't you price it over the phone?

Because the price lives in the entry points and the extent of it, and neither can be seen from a phone call. The inspection finds both, and the removal is priced there.

What happens at the inspection?

We photograph what we find, show you the photos, and price the whole correction once — the animals out, and the way in closed.
